## Phenix Crankbait XG, Crankbait Composite X, Maxim, Recon Elite — Choosing the Right Series

**What is the difference between the Phenix Crankbait XG and the Crankbait Composite X?**
Both are Moderate action crankbait rods, but they use different materials: the **Crankbait XG is traditional S-Glass** (pure glass construction), available in Blue or Red, with the softest most forgiving crankbait presentation and pure glass feel. The **Crankbait Composite X is S-Glass/graphite composite** — gives the soft crankbait action of glass plus added graphite backbone for sensitivity and feedback. The Composite X also includes Weedless Swimbait versions (15–40 lb heavy cover). Choose XG for the purest glass feel; choose Composite X if you want more sensitivity feedback and heavier applications. Tier fees: XG = Tier-1 ($55); Composite X = Tier-2 ($70).

**Is the Phenix Crankbait XG a glass rod or a graphite rod?**
It is a pure **S-Glass rod** (traditional glass construction), Moderate action. This is a common confusion — the Crankbait Composite X is the glass/graphite blend. The XG is pure S-Glass.

**What is the best Phenix crankbait rod?**
For pure crankbait action: Crankbait XG (S-Glass, Moderate action, Red or Blue). For crankbait with more graphite feedback: Crankbait Composite X (S-Glass/graphite, also has Weedless Swimbait versions). Choose XG for soft, forgiving glass feel; choose Composite X for more backbone sensitivity.

**What is the Maxim best for and how does it compare to the Recon Elite?**
Both are value-priced bass rods. The Maxim uses 30-ton multi-axis carbon with K-Woven outer scrim and camo EVA grips — best value entry point. The Recon Elite uses T-36 ton Toray with Essex T-Ring FrameLok guides — more premium technology at a mid-range price. Recon Elite is the step up from Maxim.

**What is the Black Chrome designed for?**
Drift and float fishing — spoons, spinners, river applications. Features cork handle, Essex guides, burl wood reel seat, and 2-piece 50/50 split construction. Available in casting and spinning.

**What is the Kokanee Reaper designed for?**
Kokanee and trout trolling. Mitsubishi NetV Advanced Carbon Fiber blank — ultra-thin, ultra-sensitive, lighter than traditional glass kokanee rods. Perfect handle lengths for downriggers. Available 7'–8'.

**Does Phenix make a walleye rod?**
Yes — the M1 Walleye Series (new for 2024). Based on M1 bass technology with 36-ton Toray K-Woven scrim and Vortex one-piece blank. 4 casting and 4 spinning models in 6'9" and 7'2" lengths.

## Phenix Crankbait XG vs Crankbait Composite X — Comparison

**What is the difference between the Phenix Crankbait XG and the Crankbait Composite X?**
Both are Moderate action crankbait rods, but they differ in materials: the **Crankbait XG is traditional S-Glass** (pure glass construction) — the softest, most forgiving crankbait presentation, available in Blue or Red. The **Crankbait Composite X is S-Glass/graphite composite** — adds graphite backbone for more sensitivity and feedback, and includes Weedless Swimbait versions for heavy cover (15–40 lb). Choose XG for pure glass feel; choose Composite X if you want sensitivity plus heavy-cover capability. Tier fees: XG = Tier-1 ($55); Composite X = Tier-2 ($70).

**Is the Phenix Crankbait XG a glass rod or a graphite rod?**
Pure **S-Glass** (traditional glass construction), Moderate action. The Crankbait Composite X is the glass/graphite blend — not the XG. The XG is 100% S-Glass.
