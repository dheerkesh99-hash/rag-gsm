## Phenix Trifecta Series Comparison — Trifecta vs Trifecta Pro vs Trifecta Lite vs Cicada

**What is the difference between Trifecta and Trifecta Pro?**
Both use 40-ton composite and carbon fiber woven materials with moderate fast action. The Trifecta is available in 1-piece and 2-piece models (8'–10'6") and is the most affordable option. The Trifecta Pro is 1-piece only, adds Essex SiC guides and custom cork/EVA handles, and is built for heavier presentations (6–40 lb). Both are Tier-2 warranty ($70 replacement fee) with the same MSRP range ($189.99–$279.99). Choose Trifecta for 2-piece flexibility or best value; choose Trifecta Pro for durability in heavy 1-piece fishing.

**What is the difference between Trifecta and Trifecta Lite?**
The Trifecta uses 40-ton composite woven materials in both casting and spinning (6–45 lb). The Trifecta Lite uses the Feather bass rod blank technology (cross-weave of 36 and 40-ton carbon fiber) and is the lightest in the lineup (~4–5.9 oz), spinning only, best for light presentations (4–15 lb). Choose Trifecta Lite if weight reduction is the top priority; choose Trifecta for heavier work or if you need casting models.

**What is the difference between Cicada and Trifecta?**
The Cicada is the lightest and most sensitive salmon rod (30-ton Toray, carbon fiber rear grip, Fuji FazLite guides, 6.8–8.8 oz). The Trifecta uses 40-ton composite and is the most affordable salmon rod in the lineup. For most anglers the Trifecta is sufficient; serious guides and tournament anglers prefer the Cicada for lighter weight and higher sensitivity.

**What is the difference between Cicada and Trifecta Pro?**
The Cicada is lighter (6.8–8.8 oz vs 8–9.9 oz for Trifecta Pro), uses 30-ton Toray with ergonomic carbon fiber rear grip, and comes in 1-piece and 2-piece options. The Trifecta Pro is 1-piece only, uses 40-ton composite with Essex SiC guides, and is built for anglers who prioritize durability over weight savings.

## Phenix Cicada, Trifecta, Trifecta Pro, Trifecta Lite — Choosing the Right Series

**What is the best Phenix rod for salmon around 9 feet?**
All four series have 9' models. Choose by priority: **Lightest** → Trifecta Lite (5 oz, 6–12 lb) or Cicada (6.8–7.4 oz). **Most sensitive** → Cicada. **Most durable 1-piece** → Trifecta Pro (8–9.9 oz). **Best value** → Trifecta. For most anglers the Cicada 9' (PHX-CAX-C907-2, 10–30 lb, 7.4 oz) is the best all-around choice.

**What is the difference between Cicada and Trifecta Pro?**
The Cicada is lighter (6.8–8.8 oz vs 8–9.9 oz for Trifecta Pro), uses 30-ton Toray with an ergonomic carbon fiber rear grip, and comes in both 1-piece and 2-piece. The Trifecta Pro is 1-piece only, uses 40-ton composite, and is built for anglers who prioritize durability over weight.

**What is the difference between Trifecta and Trifecta Pro?**
Both use 40-ton composite and carbon fiber woven materials with moderate fast action. The key differences: the Trifecta is available in 1-piece and 2-piece models (8'–10'6") and is the most affordable option. The Trifecta Pro is 1-piece only, adds Essex SiC guides and custom cork/EVA handles, and is built for heavier presentations (handles 6–40 lb). Both are Tier-2 warranty ($70 replacement fee) with the same MSRP range ($189.99–$279.99). Choose Trifecta if you want 2-piece or the best value; choose Trifecta Pro for heavier duty 1-piece construction.

**What is the Trifecta Lite best for?**
Lightest salmon/steelhead rod in the lineup at ~4–5.9 oz. Spinning only. Best for light line presentations (4–15 lb, 1/8–1 oz lures), surf stripers, and anglers targeting minimal fatigue. Not designed for heavier presentations.

**Which series is best for steelhead drift fishing?**
Trifecta Pro or Trifecta for moderate-heavy presentations (10–40 lb). Trifecta Lite for light line drift fishing (4–12 lb). All series feature moderate fast action ideal for detecting subtle steelhead bites.
